## Learning objectives

1. Execute every stage of the ERA framework on one concrete GMO.
2. State the inputs, questions, and outputs of each framework stage.
3. Produce a defensible problem-formulation document.
4. Recognise where assessments fail (missing stage, missing comparator, unexamined uncertainty).

## Background

Module 3 presents the ERA spine: problem formulation → hazard identification → hazard characterization → exposure assessment → risk characterization → uncertainty analysis → risk management → monitoring → communication. Reading it is easy; executing it on a real organism with incomplete information is the professional skill. This lab forces the execution with a deliberately incomplete dossier.

## Scientific principle

Each stage has defined inputs and outputs; a workflow is only as good as its weakest hand-off. Problem formulation converts policy protection goals into **assessment endpoints** and **measurement endpoints** — the step most skipped by beginners and most demanded by regulators.

## Safety

Paper exercise; no biosafety requirements.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. **Read the dossier (15 min).** Note what is present and absent.
2. **Problem formulation (30 min).** Complete worksheet Part A: protection goals → assessment endpoints → measurement endpoints → hypotheses.
3. **Hazard identification (20 min).** Part B: list candidate hazards with agent–mechanism–endpoint.
4. **Hazard & exposure characterization (30 min).** Part C: for your top two hazards, specify what data would quantify them, and what exposure pathways apply in the described receiving environment.
5. **Risk characterization sketch (20 min).** Part D: qualitative risk sentence per hazard using the likelihood × consequence × uncertainty language of Module 17.
6. **Gap analysis (15 min).** Part E: list every stage where the dossier's data are insufficient; write the one study you would commission first, with its design.
7. **Debrief (10 min).** Compare gap lists; identify the most commonly missing stage (instructor: it is usually uncertainty analysis).

## Expected results (instructor)

- Part A: protection goals should include non-target biodiversity (pollinators, natural enemies), soil function, wild-relative genetic integrity; measurement endpoints might be adult bee survival/colony strength, predator abundance in plots, outcrossing frequency at margins.
- Part B: strong entries include IP-47 toxicity to non-target insects with shared target-receptor biology; gene flow + establishment in wild grasses; allergenicity/IP-47 digestibility unknown.
- Part C: exposure = pollen dose to larvae/bees; expression values are the missing datum ("pending" — students must flag it).
- Part D: correct statements name likelihood, consequence, and uncertainty explicitly and conditionally ("low–moderate pending expression data").
- Part E: expected gaps — no expression quantification, no non-target tier-1 tests, no compositional analysis, no monitoring plan, no uncertainty analysis, no management proposal.

## Interpretation

The dossier is not unusual — real submissions have exactly these gaps at draft stage. The professional act is not rejecting it but specifying, via problem formulation, which data would change the risk estimate.

## Troubleshooting

- *Groups produce vague endpoints ("environment is safe").* Push: an endpoint must be a measurable entity + attribute (e.g., "abundance of the predator *Orius* spp. per plant").
- *Hazards duplicate the intended effect.* The intended pest effect is a benefit, not a hazard — the hazard is the same protein reaching *non*-targets.

## Questions

1. Why can expression "values pending" block the entire exposure assessment?
2. Distinguish an assessment endpoint from a measurement endpoint with your own example.
3. Which dossier absence is most likely to invalidate a food-safety conclusion?
4. Why is "no management proposal" itself a finding?

## Viva questions

1. What does problem formulation convert, into what?
2. Name the nine framework stages in order.
3. Why is uncertainty analysis a stage, not an afterthought?
4. What makes a risk hypothesis testable?

## Instructor answer key

- Q1: Exposure = dose × pathway; without expression values no dose can be estimated for any non-target or consumer.
- Q2: Assessment endpoint = the valued ecological property (e.g., "survival of native butterfly larvae"); measurement endpoint = the proxy you measure (e.g., "larval survival on pollen-dusted host leaves at 25 m").
- Q3: Compositional analysis + protein digestibility — the classic novel-food pair.
- Q4: Without management conditions the assessment cannot attach conditions (refuges, buffers) that make residual risk acceptable; "acceptable risk under conditions" is the normal regulatory outcome.
- Viva: (1) policy protection goals → operational assessment endpoints/hypotheses. (2) Problem formulation, hazard ID, hazard characterization, exposure assessment, risk characterization, uncertainty analysis, risk management, monitoring, communication. (3) It determines what confidence the conclusion can carry and what monitoring must watch. (4) It must be falsifiable by named data.

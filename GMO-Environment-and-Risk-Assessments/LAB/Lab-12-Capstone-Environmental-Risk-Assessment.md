# Lab 12 — Capstone: GMO Environmental Risk Assessment

**Activity type:** Capstone Project (student-led, multi-week)
**Duration:** 6–8 weeks (milestones) · **Level:** Advanced (course synthesis)

## Learning objectives

On completing the capstone a student can independently:

1. Select a real GMO and define the scope of a conceptual ERA for a specified receiving environment.
2. Execute the full Module 3 workflow from problem formulation to risk communication.
3. Integrate course datasets (Labs 03–10) as quantitative backbones for their own assessment.
4. Produce a professional ERA report and defend it orally.

## Background

This is the course's synthesis exercise: every module contributes one section. The chosen GMO must be **real** (a documented event/product); the receiving environment must be **specified** (not "the world"); all quantitative treatment uses the course's clearly labelled simulated data or published literature values with citations. The capstone is *conceptual* — no laboratory work, no field release, no real dossier data.

## Scientific principle

The capstone must demonstrate the course's core competence: for every hazard claimed, the student shows the chain

```text
Trait + Organism + Environment + Exposure + Evidence + Uncertainty → Risk statement
```

— and for every risk statement, at least one management/monitoring instrument (Module 18–19).

## Materials/data

- Course modules, datasets, and labs (all reusable with citation).
- Authoritative literature for the chosen GMO (see [REFERENCES.md](../REFERENCES.md)); regulator summaries are the most efficient sources.
- Report template (Section 8), presentation requirements (Section 7), rubric (Section 9).

## Safety

Conceptual exercise; no biosafety measures required. Intellectual-safety rule from Lab 11 applies: no invented data; uncertainty is carried, not hidden.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. **Week 1 — Selection & scope.** Choose a real GMO + receiving environment; submit a one-page problem formulation for instructor sign-off (guardrail: feasibility).
2. **Week 2–3 — Hazard identification.** Apply Lab 01's worksheet; produce the hazard table with mechanisms and exposure pathways (Modules 4–13 logic).
3. **Week 3–4 — Characterization.** Reuse/adapt Lab 03 (gene flow), Lab 05 (resistance if applicable), Lab 07 (composition if food/feed relevant), Lab 08 (dose–response if a protein is involved), Lab 09 (matrix), Lab 10 (uncertainty) — at least **four** quantitative elements must appear.
4. **Week 5 — Management & monitoring.** For each characterized risk: instrument, trigger, residual risk (Modules 18–20).
5. **Week 6 — Communication products.** Executive summary (≤1 page, lay-audience version); full report; presentation.
6. **Week 7–8 — Defence.** 15-minute presentation + 15-minute viva (questions drawn from the Assessment package style).

## Data tables

The report must include (at minimum):

- Hazard-identification table (Lab 01 format).
- Risk-characterization table (Lab 11 format).
- Risk matrix figure (Lab 09).
- Uncertainty/sensitivity summary (Lab 10 format: rank table + one tornado figure).
- Monitoring-trigger table (Module 19 format).

## Calculations

As required by the chosen GMO — minimum four quantitative elements from Step 3, each with stated assumptions and limitations.

## Expected results

There is no single correct conclusion. Graded quality signals:

- Hazards specific to the *chosen* organism and *specified* environment (boilerplate hazards indicate failure).
- Uncertainty stated as intervals/flags, not adjectives.
- Management instruments matched to characterized risks (no orphan risks; no instrument-free "acceptables").
- The science/policy boundary respected throughout (Lab 11 discipline).

## Interpretation

The final evidence-based conclusion must state: what is known, with what confidence; what is uncertain, and why it matters; what management closes the gap; and what monitoring would detect error. A conclusion of the form "safe" or "unsafe" without this apparatus does not pass.

## Troubleshooting (instructor guidance)

| Symptom | Intervention |
|---|---|
| GMO chosen too broad ("Bt crops") | Narrow to one event/product and one country/region |
| No quantitative backbone | Map the four required labs explicitly in supervision meetings |
| Values leaking into characterization | Apply the Lab 11 role-separation test to their draft |
| Report reads as advocacy (either direction) | Return to Module 42-style objectivity checklist; require comparator citations |

## Questions (student self-check before submission)

1. Could a reader reconstruct *which environment* you assessed from your report alone?
2. Does every hazard table row trace to at least one cited piece of evidence or an explicit data gap?
3. Is each risk statement accompanied by an instrument?
4. Is your conclusion falsifiable — what monitoring result would change it?

## Viva questions (instructor bank)

1. Justify your receiving-environment choice — what in it drove your highest-ranked risk?
2. Which two parameters in your quantitative backbone dominate your conclusions, and what would better measurement change?
3. Where did you decide something that is a policy question, and how did you flag it?
4. Which of your conclusions is most sensitive to comparator choice?
5. If your top-ranked monitoring trigger fired in year two, what happens next?

## Instructor answer key

No fixed answers; the rubric operationalises quality. For viva Q4, strong answers name a comparator study (e.g., against sprayed conventional baseline vs unsprayed) and articulate how the risk statement would shift. For Q5, strong answers connect the trigger to a pre-agreed adaptive-management action with a decision owner.

## Advanced challenge

Add a "regulatory transferability" annex: run your assessment a second time under a different framework (Module 22) and document exactly which conclusions are framework-invariant. Defend the invariance claim — it is the deepest test of the course's central distinction.

## Report template (Section 8 — required structure)

1. Executive summary (≤1 page, plain language)
2. GMO description & genetic modification (Modules 1, 4)
3. Intended trait & mechanism
4. Receiving environment specification (Module 2)
5. Problem formulation (Module 3)
6. Hazard identification (table; Modules 4–13)
7. Exposure pathways (Module 13; figure required)
8. Quantitative characterizations (≥4 elements; Labs 03/05/07/08/09/10)
9. Uncertainty analysis (Module 16; Lab 10 outputs)
10. Risk characterization (table + matrix; Module 17)
11. Risk management (instrument per risk; Module 18)
12. Monitoring plan (baseline, indicators, triggers; Module 19–20)
13. Risk communication summary (Module 21 — the lay executive summary)
14. Final evidence-based conclusion
15. References (AUTHORITATIVE SOURCES ONLY — no fabricated citations)

## Assessment rubric (Section 9 — 100 points)

| Criterion | Points | Indicators of full marks |
|---|---|---|
| Problem formulation & scope | 10 | Sharp, feasible, specified environment |
| Hazard identification quality | 15 | Specific, mechanistic, traceable to evidence |
| Quantitative backbone (≥4 elements) | 20 | Correct method, stated assumptions, honest intervals |
| Uncertainty treatment | 15 | Separated variability/uncertainty; sensitivity-informed |
| Risk characterization & matrix | 10 | Evidence-anchored placements; no false precision |
| Management & monitoring integration | 15 | Every risk has an instrument; triggers pre-agreed |
| Communication products | 10 | Lay summary accurate without sensationalism or false reassurance |
| Objectivity & science/policy discipline | 5 | Lab 11 boundary maintained; no advocacy |

**Presentation requirements (Section 7):** 15 minutes, 10–14 slides, must include the pathway figure, the risk matrix, and the monitoring-trigger table; the audience includes a "regulator" and a "reviewer" who each ask one question from the viva bank.

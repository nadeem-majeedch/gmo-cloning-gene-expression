## Grading rubrics (short/long/case/data answers)

**Short questions (3–5 sentences):** definition accuracy (40%) · example/structure (30%) · nuance — uncertainty, comparators, limits (30%). Full marks require at least one honest-limit clause.

**Long questions:** outline coverage (40%) · evidence anchoring (course cases/data, 25%) · interpretive discipline (difference≠harm; assessment≠decision — 20%) · structure/clarity (15%). Advocacy drift in either direction caps at 60%.

**Case-based:** correct chain hazard→exposure→consequence (40%) · instrument proposed (25%) · uncertainty carried (20%) · science/policy separation (15%).

**Data-interpretation:** computation correct (35%) · interpretation in ERA language (35%) · limitation named (20%) · units/design checks (10%).

**Viva:** full marks = precise one-sentence answers; half marks = correct-but-vague; zero for hazard/risk conflation or unfalsifiable claims.

## Common wrong-answer patterns (for feedback)

1. "Safe/dangerous" verdicts without exposure or uncertainty → return to Module 2 chain.
2. Treating lab hazard findings as risk conclusions → monarch case re-read.
3. Significance-without-effect-size readings → Lab 04 three-sentence drill.
4. "Zero gene flow is achievable" → decay-tail discussion, Lab 03.
5. Refuges seen as compliance-optional → Case 1 + DQ6.

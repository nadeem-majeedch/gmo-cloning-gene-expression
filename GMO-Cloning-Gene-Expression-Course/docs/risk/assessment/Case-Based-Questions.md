# Case-Based Questions — GMO Environment and Risk Assessments

**15 scenario questions with guided answers · Application → Evaluation**
*Instructor: Dr. Saira Azam, CEMB, University of the Punjab, Lahore.*

---

**CQ1.** A company proposes Bt chickpea for Pakistan, where wild *Cicer* relatives occur in some districts. Structure the problem formulation.
*Guide:* protection goals (wild *Cicer* genetic integrity, non-target lepidopterans, farm sustainability); hazards to scope: crop-to-wild gene flow (Module 6), non-target exposure via pollen/tissue (Module 8), resistance in pod-borer (Module 11); environment specification: districts with relatives, cropping calendar, pollinator fauna; analysis plan mapping each hazard to required data.

**CQ2.** A lab study shows 25% mortality of a beneficial lacewing fed Bt-treated eggs at 100× field dose. The developer claims "no risk." Assess the claim.
*Guide:* claim overreaches — tier-1 hazard finding at forced dose; correct next step: realistic-exposure semi-field/field tiers, tritrophic route analysis (does the lacewing consume intoxicated prey?), comparator (insecticide regime replaced). "No risk" is unproven *and* unproven danger — the honest state is "hazard at high dose; exposure-dependent risk under quantification."

**CQ3.** Monitoring finds transgene hybrids in a wild relative at 2 km from the nearest GM field — beyond the modelled tail. What follows?
*Guide:* do not discard the datum; re-examine model structure (long-distance mechanisms: turbulence, insect vectors, unrecorded fields, seed movement); re-estimate parameters; check consequence (fitness effect of trait in recipient — Lab 03/06 logic); update management if threshold crossed; report model uncertainty honestly (Module 16).

**CQ4.** A HT rice line escapes herbicide regimes; volunteers persist three seasons. Characterize the risk chain.
*Guide:* trait (tolerance) × organism (rice volunteers) × environment (rotations) → exposure (volunteer presence in follow-up crops) → consequence (narrowed herbicide options, seed-bank persistence — Module 7); management: rotation design, alternative chemistry, machinery hygiene; monitoring: volunteer counts with triggers.

**CQ5.** A stack (Cry1F + Cry3Bb + HT) shows no single-protein interaction in lab mixes but field refuges are not enforced. Evaluate the resistance risk profile.
*Guide:* components individually characterized; interaction low under tested conditions — state power limits (Q58 logic); dominant risk is compliance-dependent refuge failure (Case 1 lesson); risk statement must carry compliance as an exposure parameter; instruments: enforcement-linked conditions, monitoring triggers, stewardship audits — not more lab data.

**CQ6.** An assessor's Monte Carlo output: median flow 0.12%, 90% interval 0.03–0.45%; threshold 0.1%. Draft the risk statement.
*Guide:* template (Lab 10): state median/interval, dominance (decay parameter), exceedance share, response options (measure b better / manage conservatively / robust design); explicitly refuse a bare "safe/unsafe" verdict; connect to buffer-distance choice.

**CQ7.** Regulators receive two compositional datasets: 50 analytes, one at p=0.02 (fat, +4%, within conventional range). The NGO demands rejection. Advise.
*Guide:* multiplicity (expected ~2–3 false positives at α=0.05 — Lab 07 challenge); within-range + tiny magnitude + single test → flag for pattern analysis, not rejection; check consistency across sites; the decision layer may still consider it — but the assessment's characterization is "no indication of unintended compositional change" pending consistency data.

**CQ8.** A GMM producing a biocontrol enzyme is proposed for open-field spray. List the assessment elements that differ from a crop ERA.
*Guide:* survival/dispersal kinetics, population establishment/reversibility, HGT potential (Module 9/25.9 logic), containment contingency, environmental fate of cells vs proteins; monitoring: viability persistence, gene-transfer sentinels; management: application windows, buffer zones, kill-switch/auxotrophy engineering if claimed — verify.

**CQ9.** A gene-edited (SDN-1) herbicide-tolerance crop faces different regulatory tracks in two markets. Frame the scientific vs regulatory components.
*Guide:* scientific: molecular characterization (edit confirmation, off-target screen), phenotypic/compositional equivalence — the same either way; regulatory: trigger (process vs product) differs; assessors can characterize identically while regulators decide differently (Module 22) — separating the layers is the answer.

**CQ10.** Post-release year 3: resistance bioassays show 4% survivors at diagnostic dose (trigger = 2%). What does adaptive management require?
*Guide:* trigger fired → pre-agreed response, not ad-hoc debate: verify assay, increase refuge/enforcement, rotate/stack chemistry or toxin, intensify monitoring frequency, alert stewardship network (Module 19; Case 13); document decision trail; revise ERA assumption (q₀/dominance) with field data.

**CQ11.** A ministry official asks: "Is Bt cotton safe?" Draft the two-sentence scientifically defensible reply.
*Guide:* "Assessments to date, across jurisdictions, find the characterized risks acceptable under specified conditions — chiefly effective refuges and monitoring; the dominant documented risk is resistance evolution where management lapses." Then: "No assessment proves absolute safety; it quantifies risk and uncertainty, and conditions sustain acceptability." (Module 21 discipline.)

**CQ12.** An NGO publishes a map of "contamination" incidents (gene flow detections) implying ecological harm. Critique.
*Guide:* detections ≠ harm (Q25 logic): consequence depends on trait fitness effect in recipient (often neutral); distinguish crop-to-crop commingling (coexistence issue) from crop-to-wild introgression (ecological issue); demand the exposure→consequence chain before harm claims — while acknowledging transparency value of the underlying data.

**CQ13.** A developer's dossier asserts "no unintended effects" citing only compositional equivalence. Identify the gaps.
*Guide:* unintended-effect analysis requires the interpretive chain (Module 16): molecular characterization (position/pleiotropy screen), phenotypic/agronomic comparison across environments, expression profiling, and honest treatment of detected-but-benign differences; "no unintended effects" is unfalsifiable as phrased — require "no indication of unintended change beyond characterized differences."

**CQ14.** Two assessors score volunteer-persistence L=3,C=3 and L=4,C=2 — same cell, same product. What does this reveal and what is fixed?
*Guide:* identical product scores → anchors not scorers are the issue (Lab 09); fix: frequency-anchored likelihood descriptors and magnitude-anchored consequence descriptors, pre-declared; document; re-score — the matrix improves by descriptor iteration, not scorer calibration.

**CQ15.** Design the executive-summary risk paragraph (5 sentences) for a proposed GM soybean in a region with no wild relatives and glyphosate-resistant weeds already present.
*Guide:* (1) trait/organism/environment specificity; (2) gene-flow risk negligible via compatible-relative route (none present), crop-to-crop manageable by segregation; (3) dominant characterized risk: herbicide-regime resistance selection — already present locally, trait could intensify; (4) instruments: mandated rotation/mixture stewardship + resistance monitoring triggers; (5) uncertainty: volunteer dynamics across rotations — flagged with proposed measurement. Full-capstone logic in miniature.

# Viva Questions — GMO Environment and Risk Assessments

**40 rapid-response questions with model answers · oral-examination format**
*Instructor: Dr. Saira Azam, CEMB, University of the Punjab, Lahore.*

---

## Basics (Q1–Q8)

**Q1. Define risk assessment in one sentence.**
Characterizing, with stated uncertainty, what harm a GMO could cause, to what, how likely — to inform management and decisions.

**Q2. Give the four components of the conceptual risk relationship.**
Hazard, exposure, dose–response, context (conceptually combining to risk).

**Q3. What is the receiving environment?**
The ecosystem/agroecosystem into which the GMO would be released — its species, climate, practices — defining exposure contexts.

**Q4. Name the ERA stages in order.**
Problem formulation → hazard identification → hazard characterization → exposure assessment → risk characterization → uncertainty → management → monitoring → communication.

**Q5. What is an assessment endpoint?**
The valued ecological property to protect (e.g., pollinator abundance, soil function) that measurement endpoints proxy.

**Q6. What does case-by-case mean here?**
Each GMO × environment combination is assessed on its own evidence — no blanket judgments across traits or organisms.

**Q7. What is a protection goal?**
What society/regulation wants kept unharmed (biodiversity, ecosystem services) — set in problem formulation, often policy-derived.

**Q8. One-line: why assess at all when harm isn't proven?**
To decide knowingly: quantify what could go wrong, how likely, how serious — before, not after, release.

## Concepts (Q9–Q16)

**Q9. Hazard vs risk — with the glass-cobra image.**
Hazard = venomous bite potential; risk = it behind thick glass = near zero. Exposure gates the outcome.

**Q10. Why isn't "no evidence of harm" proof of safety?**
It may mean no competent search — power, endpoints, and duration determine what absence of evidence is worth.

**Q11. What makes a hazard statement "assessable"?**
Agent + mechanism + endpoint specified — data can then support or refute it.

**Q12. Difference between measurement uncertainty and variability?**
Measurement shrinks with better instruments; variability is real-world heterogeneity needing design coverage (more sites/years).

**Q13. What is a comparator and why consequential?**
The reference (near-isogenic, conventional regime) against which difference is judged — defines "different," steering all conclusions.

**Q14. Weight of evidence — two sentences.**
Integrate multiple imperfect lines; conclusions strengthen with convergence, weaken with unexplained conflict.

**Q15. What is "familiarity"?**
OECD concept: prior knowledge of organism biology and use reduces novelty-driven uncertainty — it informs, never replaces, assessment.

**Q16. Define exposure pathway.**
Route linking source (GMO/protein) to receptor: e.g., pollen → deposition → non-target larva → predator.

## Ecology (Q17–Q24)

**Q17. Name three non-target functional groups and one exposure route each.**
Pollinators (pollen/nectar), predators (prey-borne protein), soil Collembola (residue/exudates).

**Q18. What is a tritrophic route?**
Plant → herbivore → natural enemy; e.g., parasitoid developing inside Bt-intoxicated hosts.

**Q19. Why do tier-1 tests use maximum hazard?**
Screening conservatively: if even forced dose shows nothing, escalation stops; if effect appears, realistic-exposure tiers decide.

**Q20. What did the monarch case quantify?**
Field pollen densities on milkweed, per-event toxicity, phenology overlap — all far below lab-effect doses → negligible landscape risk.

**Q21. Two structural differences of GMM ERAs?**
Survival/dispersal dominance and HGT potential; reversibility/containment central.

**Q22. What is introgression's minimal condition set?**
Compatible relative + hybrid viability/fertility + backcross persistence (neutral or advantaged).

**Q23. Why is the herbicide regime, not the HT trait, the usual resistance driver?**
Selection acts where the pressure is — repeated glyphosate on weeds; the trait enabled the regime, the regime imposed the selection.

**Q24. What is an ecosystem-service framing of risk?**
Assessing harm to services (pollination, biological control, nutrient cycling) — tying endpoints to societal protection goals.

## Quantitative (Q25–Q32)

**Q25. Why Wilson over Wald intervals?**
Better small-n/extreme-proportion behaviour — standard in survival/hybridization counts.

**Q26. Interpret f(d)=a·e^(−bd).**
Edge frequency a decaying at rate b; threshold distance d* = ln(a/threshold)/b for management design.

**Q27. What does an S-shaped resistance curve reveal?**
Selection on rare recessives is weak early, compounding later — refuges exploit exactly that window.

**Q28. LC50 vs NOEC — one sentence each.**
LC50: dose killing half the cohort (hazard parameter). NOEC: highest tested concentration without observed effect (design-limited, falling from regulatory favour).

**Q29. Margin of exposure formula and use.**
MOE = (effect dose, e.g., LC50) / (estimated field exposure) — ratios >>1 support negligible-risk statements, with uncertainty.

**Q30. What is TOST?**
Two one-sided tests proving the difference CI lies within pre-declared equivalence margins — the correct "equivalence" tool.

**Q31. Monte Carlo output is a distribution. What's reported?**
Median plus interval (e.g., 90%) and exceedance probabilities against thresholds — never just the mean.

**Q32. What does a tornado plot show?**
Output swing as each input moves low→high bound — visual sensitivity ranking.

## Regulation & ethics (Q33–Q40)

**Q33. Cartagena Annex III in one sentence.**
Principles for LMO risk assessment: case-by-case, science-based, step-wise, transparent, with precaution considerations.

**Q34. Which three US agencies and their domains?**
USDA-APHIS (plant pest), EPA (PIPs/pesticides), FDA (food/feed).

**Q35. EU process-trigger vs Canada product-trigger — one example each of divergence.**
EU: gene-edited small deletions captured as GMOs. Canada: a conventionally-bred novel trait captured while the edit might not be.

**Q36. Pakistan's three committee tiers?**
NBC (approvals/policy), TAC (technical review), IBCs (institutional oversight).

**Q37. PMEM vs general surveillance?**
Case-specific monitoring targets ERA-identified hazards; general surveillance watches broadly for the unanticipated — both under pre-agreed triggers.

**Q38. Why keep benefits out of the safety characterization?**
Conflating them smuggles policy into hazard science; benefits belong to the separate decision layer.

**Q39. One mark of advocacy drift in a student report?**
Conclusions outrunning evidence in either direction — e.g., "safe" without uncertainty, or "dangerous" without exposure.

**Q40. The course motto?**
Trait + organism + environment + exposure + evidence + uncertainty — every GMO evaluated as its own case.

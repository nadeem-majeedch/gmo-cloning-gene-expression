# Lab 11 — Case-Study Regulatory Assessment

**Activity type:** Case Study (role-play simulation)
**Duration:** 3 hours · **Level:** Advanced

---

## Learning objectives

1. Apply the full ERA workflow (Module 3) to a novel dossier under time constraints.
2. Separate scientific assessment outputs from regulatory decision inputs — and experience the tension.
3. Draft the core sections of a risk-assessment report: problem formulation, characterization, uncertainty statement.
4. Defend an evidence-based position against informed counter-argument.

## Background

Modules 22–23 showed that jurisdictions reach different decisions on similar evidence. This lab makes students *live* that process: a fictional-but-realistic dossier is assessed by competing roles (assessor, developer, public-interest reviewer, regulator), each bound by the same evidence. The outcome is not scripted — and the debrief examines why.

## Scientific principle

The assessment/decision boundary (Module 22):

- **Assessors** characterise risk and uncertainty with no decision authority.
- **Regulators** set acceptable-risk thresholds, conditions, and burdens of proof — policy-laden choices.
- **Stakeholders** contribute values and local knowledge — legitimate inputs to *decision*, not to *characterization*.

Role instructions enforce this separation; violations are graded.

## Materials/data

- **Dossier (provided by instructor or generated from the course):** a fictional stacked-trait Bt + herbicide-tolerant (HT) maize proposed for release in a region with irrigated maize, smallholder refuges unlikely to be enforced, and a sexually compatible wild relative present. Dossier includes: molecular characterization summary, compositional table (Lab 07 style), three non-target studies (Lab 04 style), gene-flow data (Lab 03 style), resistance model outputs (Lab 05 style), and a developer-proposed monitoring plan.
- Role cards (below), the 12-section template from Module 23, course Cheat Sheet.
- No real dossiers or proprietary data are used.

## Safety

No biosafety concerns (paper exercise). Professional-integrity rule: no role may invent data; uncertainty must be stated, not papered over.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. **Formation (10 min).** Groups of 4: Assessor (A), Developer (D), Public-interest reviewer (P), Regulator (R).
2. **Independent reading (25 min).** Each reads the dossier and lists: strongest evidence, weakest evidence, missing studies.
3. **Assessor stage (30 min).** A drafts problem formulation + hazard list; D and P each submit their top three concerns; A integrates (no decisions allowed).
4. **Characterization stage (30 min).** Classrooms' Lab 03/05/09 outputs are cited as the quantitative backbone; A produces the risk-characterization table with uncertainty flags.
5. **Decision stage (30 min).** R receives: A's characterization, D's proposed conditions, P's objections. R drafts a decision with conditions and a monitoring trigger table.
6. **Public hearing (20 min).** 3-minute statements from D, P, A; R answers using only the record.
7. **Debrief (25 min).** Compare decisions across groups; map divergences to assessment differences vs value differences.

## Expected results

Groups typically converge on the hazard list (the science is shared) but diverge on: refuge enforcement as a condition, the acceptability of monitoring-triggered withdrawal, and the standard of proof for the wild-relative pathway — precisely the assessment/decision boundary the lab exists to teach.

## Interpretation

- Divergence is not failure: the debrief question is *which* divergence came from evidence and *which* from values. Both are legitimate; only one belongs in the characterization table.
- A decision with conditions is not a "weaker yes" — conditions (refuges, monitoring triggers, review dates) are the regulator's principal risk-management instruments (Module 18).
- The reviewer's role is not to oppose but to test whether the characterization's uncertainties are honestly carried into the decision record.

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Regulator rubber-stamps developer | Role capture | Require R to cite one dossier weakness and one condition responding to it |
| Assessor makes the decision | Boundary violation | Instructor intervention; re-issue role cards |
| Debate loops on "is GMO safe" | Framing collapse | Return to the hazard table; "safe" is not an assessable claim (Lab 01) |

## Questions

1. Which dossier weakness did the developer have the strongest incentive to minimise, and did the process surface it?
2. What condition could the regulator set that does not depend on future grower compliance?
3. Where did your group's decision rest on values rather than evidence? Name it explicitly.
4. Which monitoring trigger, if pre-agreed, would most change the risk profile itself?

## Viva questions

1. Why is the assessor's report allowed to conclude "uncertain" while the regulator's record may not?
2. What is the difference between a condition and a recommendation?
3. Why do real agencies publish assessment reports but keep deliberations separate?
4. Name one way stakeholder input legitimately changes an ERA.

## Instructor answer key

- Q1: Typically refuge enforceability (it undercuts the resistance-management story); the public-interest reviewer's checklist should force it onto the record.
- Q2: e.g., buffer strips on developer-managed seed-production fields, or binding monitoring triggers with automatic suspension — conditions that do not rely on distributed compliance.
- Q3: Common honest answers: acceptable-risk threshold for the wild-relative pathway; whether economic effects on smallholders count as "consequence." Both are policy judgments; grading rewards naming them.
- Q4: Legitimately: local knowledge of wild relatives/land use can refine exposure characterization; values can set decision thresholds — but not edit the hazard table.

## Advanced challenge

Rewrite the decision for a *different* jurisdiction (choose: US Coordinated Framework, EU Directive 2001/18, Pakistan NBC) with the same dossier. List exactly which parts of the assessment carried over unchanged and which decision elements changed — the empirical map of science vs policy in this course.

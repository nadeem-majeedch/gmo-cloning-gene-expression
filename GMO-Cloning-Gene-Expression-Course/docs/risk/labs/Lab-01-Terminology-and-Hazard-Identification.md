# Lab 01 — GMO Risk-Assessment Terminology and Hazard Identification

**Activity type:** Case Study / Simulation (no wet lab)
**Duration:** 2 hours · **Level:** Beginner

---

## Learning objectives

By the end of this practical you will be able to:

1. Define hazard, exposure, risk, uncertainty, risk management, and risk communication without notes.
2. Distinguish hazards from risks in statements commonly heard in public debate.
3. Identify potential hazards for a described GMO using a structured worksheet.
4. Express identified hazards as testable statements (mechanism, exposure pathway, consequence).

## Background

Public discussion of GMOs constantly confuses "hazard" (a thing that could cause harm) with "risk" (the chance × consequence of that harm under real conditions). Professional risk assessment begins by separating them — and by phrasing every identified hazard so that evidence could, in principle, support or refute it.

## Scientific principle

The conceptual chain taught in Module 2:

```text
Hazard + Exposure + Dose/Response + Context → Risk (conceptually)
```

A hazard statement has three parts: **agent** (what could cause harm), **mechanism** (how), and **endpoint** (what harm). If any part is missing, the statement is not yet assessable.

## Materials/data

- Printed/quoted public statements (provided in the worksheet below).
- The hazard-identification worksheet (Section 8).
- Course Cheat Sheet for terminology reference.
- No laboratory equipment, living material, or biohazard is involved.

## Safety

This is a **paper exercise**: no biosafety precautions are required. The relevant professional safety here is intellectual: never treat an advocacy statement as evidence.

## Step-by-step workflow

1. **Terminology drill (15 min).** Without notes, write your own one-sentence definitions of: hazard, exposure, risk, uncertainty, risk management, risk communication. Compare with the Cheat Sheet; correct your own.
2. **Statement sorting (25 min).** Classify each statement below as: (H) hazard claim, (R) risk claim, (M) management claim, (C) communication/perception claim.
3. **Hazard re-writing (25 min).** For each (H) statement, rewrite it as a complete hazard statement (agent–mechanism–endpoint).
4. **Worksheet (30 min).** Complete the hazard-identification worksheet for the crop described in Section 7.
5. **Peer review (15 min).** Swap worksheets; mark each hazard statement assessable or not, with reasons.
6. **Debrief (10 min).** Instructor aggregates hazards; class maps them to course modules.

## Calculations

None in this lab. (Quantitative treatments begin in Lab 03.)

## Expected results

- Statements 1, 5, 7 are hazard claims (though statement 1 lacks a mechanism/endpoint — the rewrite step exposes this).
- Statements 2, 6 are risk/management hybrid claims depending on reading; defensible either way **if** justified.
- Statements 3, 6 contain management actions.
- Statements 4, 8 are communication/perception claims — legitimate topics (Module 21), but not science claims.

## Interpretation

A hazard claim without exposure or context is not a risk claim — and a perception claim is not evidence about either. Most public "GMO debates" are people arguing about different boxes of this taxonomy at cross purposes.

## Troubleshooting

Not applicable (paper exercise). If groups disagree on a classification, the productive question is: *what evidence would move this statement from one box to another?*

## Questions

1. Rewrite statement 1 as a fully assessable hazard statement.
2. Why is statement 2 a risk claim while statement 1 is a hazard claim?
3. Give one example where a communication claim influenced real regulatory outcomes in this course's case studies.
4. Which of your worksheet hazards maps to Module 6, and how would you make it testable?

## Viva questions

1. Define "hazard" and "risk" in one sentence each.
2. Why does "absence of evidence is not evidence of absence" matter for hazard identification?
3. Name the three parts of a complete hazard statement.
4. Why is "GM crops are dangerous" not an assessable statement?

## Instructor answer key

- Q1 example: "The Cry1Ac protein (agent), when ingested by non-target lepidopteran larvae feeding on pollen-dusted plants near Bt cotton fields (mechanism/exposure), may reduce their survival (endpoint)."
- Q2: Statement 2 contains an exposure estimate (field-edge pollen density) and a dose comparison — that is risk logic; statement 1 identifies a property only.
- Q3: StarLink (Case 5) — feed-only approval collapsed under public/consumer exposure fears; monarch episode (Case 2) — perception preceded exposure data.
- Q4: "Dangerous" names no agent, mechanism, or endpoint; nothing about it can be supported or refuted by data as phrased.

## Advanced challenge

Take the class's best hazard from the worksheet and design the *minimum* observation or experiment that would falsify it. State the falsification criterion numerically.
